Gradient descent vs. Nesterov acceleration

Minimizing a strongly convex objective f(x)=logΣexp(⟨aᵢ,x⟩+cᵢ) + ½(m₁x₁²+m₂x₂²). The log-sum-exp term bends the level sets away from ellipses; the quadratic term sets the strong-convexity floor. Both methods share the same start and the same step size α=1/L. The surface is the objective itself — height is function value — so you can watch both methods slide down the curved valley.

The log-sum-exp behaves like a smooth max of the affine pieces ⟨aᵢ,x⟩+cᵢ, so curvature varies from point to point: the valley is a curved trough rather than a quadratic bowl, gradient directions rotate along the path, and the trajectories bend. Conditioning lives in the anisotropic quadratic — the κ slider shrinks m₁, flattening the low-curvature direction and stretching the trough. The L, μ and momentum β=(√κ−1)/(√κ+1) used are measured from the Hessian's extreme eigenvalues over this window, not assumed, and x★ (the ✕) is located by Newton's method. Height on the surface is √((f−f★)/span) rather than f itself: the raw surface is steep enough at the rim that its near wall would hide the valley floor at any usable camera angle, and the square root is monotone so it moves no level set. Gradient descent uses x₊ = x − α∇f(x); Nesterov adds y = x + β(x−x₋), x₊ = y − α∇f(y). On the log-scale panel the two slopes are the story: descent decays like (1−1/κ)ᵏ, Nesterov like (1−1/√κ)ᵏ — the √κ speedup. The faint dashed lines are those textbook worst-case rates; the empirical curves can outrun their own bounds, so read the gap between the two slopes rather than the absolute offset. One honest caveat: log-sum-exp has a bounded, non-vanishing gradient far from x★, which drives quick early progress, so the acceleration gap here is real but modest — push κ up to make the √κ separation clearer.
