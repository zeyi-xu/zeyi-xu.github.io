An adaptive rule sets its own step from what it sees each iteration, so it rides the fast edge without you hunting for it. The Hessian of f(x)=½(κx₁²+x₂²) is the constant diag(κ,1), so the smoothness and strong-convexity constants are exact: L=κ, μ=1, condition number κ=L/μ. Gradient descent x₊ = x − η∇f(x) shrinks coordinate i by (1−ηλᵢ), so it is stable only when 0 < η < 2/L. The step slider reads in units of that limit: η = s·(2/L), and at s=1 the steep coordinate flips past −1 and diverges. The catch is that the flat coordinate only moves at rate 1−2s/κ, so to make progress you must push s toward the cliff — the useful band is a sliver of width ~1/κ just below s=1, centered on the optimum η★ = 2/(μ+L) with rate ρ = (κ−1)/(κ+1) (the dashed line on the log panel; f falls as ρ²ᵏ). Adaptive rules remove the search. The Polyak step ηₖ = (f(xₖ)−f★)/‖∇f(xₖ)‖² keeps GD's direction but sets the length from the gap itself — nothing changes except the step, and it tracks the optimal fixed rate automatically (it does need to know f★). AdaGrad Gᵢ ⟵ Gᵢ + (∂ᵢf)², xᵢ₊ = xᵢ − η₀ ∂ᵢf /(√Gᵢ+ε) gives each coordinate its own shrinking step η₀/√Gᵢ (the lower panel), which drops faster on the steep coordinate and effectively pre-conditions the geometry — it can beat any scalar fixed step early, though on a pure quadratic its √k decay eventually slows it, and the rescaling is tied to the coordinate frame. That last method is exactly mirror descent under a data-dependent metric — the Bregman term built from diag(√Gₖ) — the same adaptive-geometry idea as the mirror-descent page, here read as a self-adjusting step. Faint dashed line on the log panel is the optimal fixed-step rate ρ²ᵏ; drag κ to widen the gap and watch the fixed sliver thin. Drag the surface to orbit; click it to move the start.
